## PART 1: HISTORICAL LORE DATABASE

The May 13, 1985 MOVE bombing remains one of the most tragic and shocking chapters in modern American history. The event resulted in the deaths of 11 people (including 5 children), left 250 citizens homeless, and leveled an entire middle-class Black neighborhood after a police helicopter dropped a military-grade satchel bomb onto a residential row house[cite: 1]. This structured database compiles historical milestones, survivor testimony, and archival documentation of the tragedy[cite: 1].

### Chronological Timeline of Conflict Escalation

* **1973 — Founding of MOVE**
  Founded by Vincent Leaphart (who took the name John Africa), MOVE emerges as a Black naturalist and revolutionary organization in West Philadelphia[cite: 1]. They advocate for a complete rejection of modern technology, industrialization, and municipal systems, practicing communal living, strict veganism, and animal rights advocacy[cite: 1].

* **August 8, 1978 — The Powelton Village Shootout**
  Following a year-long armed standoff over city housing violations at MOVE’s original Powelton Village headquarters, a shootout erupts[cite: 1]. Police Officer James Ramp is killed[cite: 1]. Nine MOVE members (the "MOVE 9") are arrested and later given 30-to-100-year prison sentences—a focal point for future MOVE protests[cite: 1].

* **1982 — Relocation to Osage Avenue**
  The remaining members of MOVE relocate to a brick row house at 6221 Osage Avenue in a middle-class, residential neighborhood[cite: 1]. To protest the imprisonment of the MOVE 9, members construct a fortified rooftop bunker and broadcast political messages through high-powered loudspeakers day and night[cite: 1].

* **May 12, 1985 — The Eviction Orders**
  Complaints from neighbors regarding the loudspeakers, sanitation issues, and neighborhood disruption prompt Philadelphia Mayor Wilson Goode and Police Commissioner Gregory Sambor to secure eviction warrants[cite: 1]. Nearby residents are evacuated from their homes on Mother’s Day, anticipating a police operation[cite: 1].

* **May 13, 1985 — The Bombing and Conflagration**
  At 5:35 p.m., after a day-long tactical assault involving 10,000 rounds of ammunition, tear gas, and water cannons fails to dislodge the group, a state police helicopter drops a bomb onto the rooftop bunker[cite: 1]. Officials choose to "let the fire burn" to destroy the bunker, causing a massive blaze that destroys 61 neighborhood homes[cite: 1].

### Survivor Perspectives & Firsthand Accounts

Only two individuals inside 6221 Osage Avenue survived the inferno: Ramona Africa (an adult member) and Michael Ward (known then as Birdie Africa, a 13-year-old child)[cite: 1]. Their documented experiences highlight the horrific final moments inside the house[cite: 1].

> **Ramona Africa on the Escalation:**
> *"The house shook... we start feeling the heat and the smoke, which we knew wasn't tear gas. We knew there was a fire... We all decided we was coming out and we were bringing the children out of the house and hollering to the police, 'We're coming out, we're bringing the children out!' And their response was gunshots."*[cite: 1]

Ramona was immediately arrested upon escaping the burning building[cite: 1]. She served seven years in prison on charges of riot and conspiracy, making her the only individual involved in the entire incident to face criminal incarceration[cite: 1].

Michael Ward (Birdie Africa), the 13-year-old child survivor, was rescued from a water-filled alley with severe third-degree burns[cite: 1]. His mother, Rhonda Africa, perished in the fire[cite: 1]. Decades of legal hearings confirmed that the children inside were choking on heavy smoke while huddled in a subterranean garage area, facing intense gunfire when attempting to flee[cite: 1].
